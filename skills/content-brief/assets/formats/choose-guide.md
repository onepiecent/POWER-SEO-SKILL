# Format: choose-guide

Use when: reader_need = choose ("X vs Y", "best X for Y", "how to choose X").
Reader job: decide between options and feel sure of the decision.

## Skeleton
- H1: primary keyword + decision angle.
- Verdict first (2-4 sentences): which option for which person, in plain words.
- H2: What matters when choosing (3-5 criteria, ranked)
- H2: Side-by-side comparison (table: criteria x options)
- H2: Who should pick which (short profiles)
- H2: What we looked at / how we compared (method; only real tests)
- H2: Drawbacks of each option
- H2: FAQs
- Byline, "Last updated", sources.

## Rules
- Explain why something is recommended, with first-hand or primary-source evidence (Google's review guidance: evidence, measurements, benefits AND drawbacks).
- Compare against real alternatives, including options we do not sell, when that helps the reader decide.
- Do not invent test results. If no testing happened, say the guide is based on documented specifications and name the sources.

## Experience prompts
- What differences have we seen in real orders (durability, fit, print quality) for these options? Photos or measurements we can show.
- What do customers regret choosing? What do support tickets say?
- What can the product team confirm about materials and print methods? (documents, not memory)

## Product-slot guidance
- Slot only when naming a specific option the reader may want to see; at most one per option.
- Slot syntax: [PRODUCT-SLOT: product idea | context: reader situation | why: why it helps this reader]
