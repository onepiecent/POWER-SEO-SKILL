# Format: gift-guide

Use when: reader_need = inspire/choose with an occasion, recipient or interest ("mother's day gifts for grandma", "gifts for nurses").
Reader job: choose a gift they feel confident about for ONE specific person, within a budget, without wasting time.

## Skeleton
- H1: primary keyword + a concrete angle (who it is for / what makes the list different). No keyword stuffing.
- Intro (BLUF, 2-3 sentences): who this is for, how the ideas were chosen, and the one-line answer ("If you only read one thing: ...").
- Quick picks (optional table): budget range x personality, with one idea per cell.
- H2: How to choose (3-5 criteria that matter for THIS recipient, not generic gift advice)
- H2 groups by reason or personality (3-6 groups, e.g. "For the grandma who loves her garden"). Under each, H3 per idea:
  - what it is, why it fits this person (the reader's problem it solves), who it suits and who it does not, practical details (sizing, personalization limits, how long it lasts)
- H2: Gifts that are not things (a card message, a shared experience) - adds real value beyond products
- H2: Common mistakes to avoid
- H2: FAQs (3-5 real questions from People Also Ask / support tickets)
- Close: short, no hard sell. Byline, "Last updated" date, sources.

## Rules
- Every idea needs a reason it fits. A bare list of products is commodity content.
- Show drawbacks honestly (e.g. "personalized items cannot be returned if the name is misspelled").
- Budgets as ranges the reader can act on; never state live prices (commercial team owns prices).
- "Best" only with criteria the reader can see. No "tested" claims unless real testing is evidenced.
- Stats and surveys need a primary source (NRF, ONS, etc.); otherwise [DATA NEEDED: ...].
- US or UK spelling and vocabulary throughout (mom/mum, personalized/personalised).

## Experience prompts
- What do customers most often personalize for this recipient or occasion? (support and design team)
- Which mistakes cause disappointment (photo quality, text length, sizing)? What do we tell customers to avoid?
- What does a good personalization look like? Examples we have permission to show.
- Which of these ideas does our team actually give? (named person, one honest sentence)
- Timing and delivery facts: ONLY from operations with a date. Never guess cut-off dates.

## Product-slot guidance
- At most one slot per idea, only where the idea itself is a product.
- None in the intro; no more than about 60% of ideas carry a slot; at least some ideas stand without any product.
- Slot syntax: [PRODUCT-SLOT: product idea | context: reader situation | why: why it helps this reader]
